# Pitch-Evidence Rules

Strict policy for every public statement about Acrevia's validation — on
stage, in the submission, on the landing page, in README, in conversation with
judges. The rule set exists because the temptation at pitch time is exactly
the sin Issue #20 forbids: turning hope into evidence.

**Prime directive: if it is not in the evidence log, it cannot be said.**

## The rules

### R1 — Never invent quotations

No fabricated quotes, testimonials, or paraphrases presented as quotes. A
quotation mark in public material requires: quote permission = yes in the
session header, the exact stored words, and the EV id.

### R2 — "Partner" requires agreement

"Design partner" and "pilot" require logged agreement with the participant —
a real yes, in a recorded follow-up, not a friendly nod at the end of a call.
Below that, only the labels **interviewed** and **tester** exist. "Partner"
alone, "customer", "user of Acrevia" (for someone who sat through one demo),
and implied logos/names are banned.

### R3 — Use the relationship vocabulary exactly

interviewed → tester → pilot → design partner (criteria in `README.md`).
Public wording follows the label: "we interviewed three pastors", "two
professionals tested the product", "one congregation is piloting it" — only
when the criteria are met and logged.

### R4 — Stated intent is not observed action

"They said they would take it to their board" (rung 7) may never become
"churches are taking this to their boards" (rung 8). Observed action requires
observation: a dated artifact, a meeting, a professional contact, recorded in
the evidence log's follow-through field. Numbers for rung 7 and rung 8 are
always reported separately (IM-12).

### R5 — No housing-created claims

Acrevia has not created housing. Housing-capacity numbers only when computed
from real properties with explicit assumptions, labeled preliminary.

### R6 — No replacement claims

Acrevia does not replace architects, planners, land-use attorneys, developers,
lenders, or formal feasibility studies — say so proactively in any pitch that
shows an output. The honest frame is acceleration of the *first* decision.

### R7 — Quotes are consented and documented

Preserve exact words only when consented (R1) and documented (timestamp + EV
id). Participants may withdraw; withdrawn quotes are marked, not deleted, and
never reused.

### R8 — Every metric is traceable

Every number in any pitch maps to an evidence-log entry or a metrics-table row
(IM-id) whose value was produced by a logged session. Before any pitch, build
the audit table:

| Claim on slide | Evidence (EV/IM/OBJ/PC id) | Ladder rung |
| --- | --- | --- |
| (each line of the pitch) | (id) | (1–9) |

A claim with an empty Evidence cell is cut or downgraded to explicit-hypothesis
wording ("we believe…", "we expect to test…").

### R9 — Anonymity survives the pitch

No participant names, org names, or identifying property details in public
material without named-permission = yes. "A board member of a mid-size
congregation" is the ceiling without consent.

### R10 — No unmeasured numbers, no best-of cherry-picking

Durations are medians with ranges and capture modes (LIVE/CACHED/FIXTURE), from
pre-warmed runs, per the property-test protocol. Percentages carry their
denominators. One great run among five is reported as one of five.

### R11 — Downgrade language under uncertainty

When evidence is partial, the wording downgrades automatically:
"we believe" (rung 1) → "public research shows" (rung 2) → "practitioners told
us" (rung 3) → "in tested sessions" (rung 4+) → "this changed our product"
(rung 5) → "a user found it useful" (rung 6) → "they said they would take it to
their board" (rung 7) → "they did" (rung 8) → "we are working with them
ongoing" (rung 9). Never skip a rung in the phrasing.

## Pre-pitch audit (run before every public cut)

1. Build the R8 audit table; cut or downgrade every unsupported line.
2. Read the objection log aloud: can we state our strongest unresolved
   objection before a judge finds it?
3. Check IM-12: are stated vs observed actions still separated everywhere,
   including slides and spoken word?
4. Check every quotation mark against EV ids and permissions.
5. Search the deck/script for the banned words: *partner, customer, replaces,
   ends homelessness, saves lives, users love, seamless, revolutionary*.
6. Verify the README counters and metrics table match the evidence log.

## Approved framing (safe defaults)

- Direct impact sentence: "Acrevia helps faith communities move potentially
  viable housing projects from conviction to a credible first development
  decision."
- Honest fallback (always available): "Validation is underway; here is exactly
  what we have measured so far and what remains open."
- The strongest pitch line this kit can ever produce is Issue #20's shape —
  tested with real roles, measured on a real property, challenged by a
  professional, changed because of it — with every bracket filled from the log.
